# Draft response to Reviewer 1, comment 6

We thank the reviewer for highlighting the inconsistent yield results and the need for a more informative explanation. The original yield trajectories assess route-dependent remaining pools and do not by themselves compare predictions for the same substrates. We therefore ran a matched common-test replay for yield and ee using 20 overlapping held-out splits per catalyst, three shared starting pairs per split, five matched label budgets and the existing frozen acquisition rule. The Fe(CMC-Por)Cl replay is restricted to the aryl cohort.

For Fe(P7)Cl, source-order, uncertainty–diversity and random yield MAEs were 21.11, 18.28 and 20.85 percentage points; uncertainty–diversity was lower in 17/20 splits against each comparator. For Fe(CMC-Por)Cl aryl substrates, the corresponding values were 24.38, 24.99 and 24.76, favouring uncertainty–diversity in 8/20 and 6/20 splits, respectively. The same-test ee results are reported in the revised SI. These small overlapping splits show variable outcomes and do not support a universal yield benefit.

We also examined how the acquisition score is constructed. Its uncertainty term combines model disagreement with molecular novelty, so the full score gives considerable weight to structural coverage and is not a calibrated estimate of yield prediction error. At a six-label budget, the ee- and yield-trained acquisition routes selected on average 2.52 and 2.98 of the same four additional substrates for Fe(P7)Cl and Fe(CMC-Por)Cl, respectively. This partial overlap is consistent with endpoint-independent structural information influencing the route, but it does not establish the cause of the yield results. Isolated yield can also reflect reaction and isolation factors beyond those probed by the descriptors, yet our data do not identify a particular chemical mechanism. We now make these limits explicit, report both the favourable and unfavourable results, and restrict our claims about the strategy’s usefulness for yield.
