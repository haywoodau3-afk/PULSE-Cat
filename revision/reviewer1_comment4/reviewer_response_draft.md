# Draft response to Reviewer 1 comment 4

We thank the reviewer for highlighting the importance of extrapolation to new substrate families. We have expanded the discussion and limitations to explain that the 10.3% family-held-out reduction corresponds to 1.07 ee points (MAE 10.406 to 9.334), and that this modest aggregate gain does not establish reliable prediction for a new family, catalyst or reaction class. The 31.9% target-held-out and 10.3% family-held-out results use different splits and fitted models, so the numerical gap is descriptive and is not interpreted as a statistically significant difference between protocols.

To examine how the aggregate result depends on the composition of the cohort, we analysed the archived paired Elastic Net predictions for all 38 substrates in 17 families. Pose features lowered family MAE in 11 families and raised it in six. Equal weighting of family MAEs gave 11.99 versus 9.84 ee points for structure-only and structure plus pose. The largest family contains 12 substrates and showed the opposite direction, with MAEs of 5.25 and 6.44. Nine of the 17 families contain a single substrate. These post hoc summaries reveal heterogeneity and sensitivity to family weighting; they are not independent validation or population-level significance tests. The complete family-level results and protocol are now in the Supporting Information.

We have revised the abstract, Results and Discussion, limitations and conclusion to temper the extrapolation claims. Family holdout concerns previously withheld substrate families within the Fe(P7)Cl dataset, whereas generalisation to a new catalyst, reaction class or prospective scope remains untested. Additional independent datasets containing more examples per family and prospective evaluation are needed to establish how the representation performs beyond the observed scope.
